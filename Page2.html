FSAI’s role

Some of the key areas where the FSAI is participating are:

  1. Food Production
  2. Food Reformulation
  3. Food Donation
  4. EU Discussions on Sustainable Food Systems
  5. Food Production Quality Assurance Schemes
  6. Food Packaging

Simple Explanation

Food Production

Sustainability initiatives aim to reduce the greenhouse gas emissions associated with food production. While animal feed additives are approved based on safety assessments carried out by the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), changes to animal digestion can affect the safety of food products. The Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI) works with the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) of the United Nations to assess the food safety impacts of environmental inhibitors used to lower emissions.

The use of organic waste as fertiliser is an example of a circular economy practice that helps recycle valuable nutrients. However, it can also pose risks to crops and livestock if it is not managed correctly. The FSAI contributes to the development of national guidelines and oversight systems to ensure these sustainable practices are carried out safely while protecting public health.

Food Reformulation

Ireland introduced a voluntary food reformulation programme to improve the nutritional quality of foods available to consumers. The initiative, outlined in *A Roadmap for Food Product Reformulation in Ireland*, runs from 2021 to 2025 and focuses on reducing the levels of calories, saturated fat, sugar, and salt in commonly consumed foods. By encouraging healthier food products, the programme supports both public health and a more sustainable food system.

The Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI), working alongside Healthy Ireland, plays a key role in delivering this initiative. The FSAI collaborates with food manufacturers to encourage the reformulation of products, helping to make healthier choices more widely available while supporting long-term sustainability and improved nutrition across Ireland.

Food Donation

Reducing food waste is an important part of building a more sustainable food system, and redistributing surplus food is one way to achieve this. Across Ireland, social enterprises and charities collect excess food from businesses and provide it to people who need it. However, any donated food must remain safe to eat, be of suitable quality, and be stored, transported, and handled correctly.

The Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI) supports these efforts by leading national initiatives to reduce food waste. It has also developed guidance for both food businesses that donate surplus food and organisations that receive it. These guidelines help ensure that food redistribution is carried out safely while reducing unnecessary waste and supporting communities.

EU Discussions on Sustainable Food Systems

The Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI) contributes to the development of food policies by representing Ireland in European discussions on sustainable food systems. As part of Ireland's delegation, the FSAI provides expert advice to the European Commission through the EU Expert Group supporting the Farm to Fork Strategy. This helps ensure that food safety is considered when shaping future food and sustainability policies across Europe.

The FSAI also contributes to the development of the EU Food Systems Dashboard, which provides data on a range of food system indicators. This resource allows governments and stakeholders to monitor food safety and sustainability performance over time and compare progress between EU Member States.

Food Production Quality Assurance Schemes

Many Irish farmers and food producers take part in the Bord Bia Quality Assurance Schemes, which promote high standards of food production and sustainability. The Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI) works closely with Bord Bia by providing expert advice through its Technical Advisory Committees. This involvement helps ensure that food safety remains a key priority as the quality assurance schemes continue to develop and support more sustainable farming and food production practices.

Food Packaging

Efforts to improve sustainability in the food sector also focus on reducing the environmental impact of packaging. Packaging plays an important role in protecting food and maintaining safety, but certain materials or improper use can also create risks for contamination or reduced food quality.

In Ireland, the Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI) is the official authority responsible for regulating food contact materials. This means it oversees the safety of all materials that come into contact with food, helping to ensure that packaging is both safe for consumers and aligned with more sustainable environmental practices.
